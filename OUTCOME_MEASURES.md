# Outcome Measures — Validated Tools Used at Recovery TLV

## Purpose

Objective outcome measurement is a non-negotiable component of the clinical decision system. Subjective improvement alone cannot justify treatment continuation. This document lists the validated outcome measures used to track progress and inform decisions.

## General Principles

1. **Baseline before treatment** — Every measure is taken at initial assessment before any intervention
2. **Repeated at defined intervals** — Re-measured at trial window end (session 4) and at each continuation decision point
3. **Beyond measurement error** — Change must exceed the instrument's minimal detectable change (MDC) and, where a value has been published, its minimal clinically important difference (MCID)
4. **Condition-specific + generic** — Use at least one condition-specific and one generic measure per case

## Physical Measures

### Range of Motion (ROM)
- **Tool:** Goniometer, inclinometer
- **Application:** All musculoskeletal conditions with ROM limitations
- **Threshold:** a measurable improvement from baseline, greater than the instrument's MDC
- **Decision link:** ROM improvement is a primary CONTINUE criterion

### Strength Testing
- **Tool:** Manual muscle testing (MMT), handheld dynamometer, 1RM estimation
- **Application:** Post-surgical, weakness-related dysfunction, tendinopathy
- **Threshold:** Dynamometry or repetition testing: a measurable improvement from baseline, greater than the instrument's MDC. MMT is ordinal: record the grade as supporting information only
- **Decision link:** Strength gains support CONTINUE, stasis suggests hypothesis revision

## Functional Performance Tests

| Test | What It Measures | Population | Reported MCID / criterion |
|------|-----------------|------------|------|
| Timed Up and Go (TUG) | Mobility, balance, fall risk | Geriatric, post-surgical | Major clinically important improvement 0.8–1.4 seconds in hip OA (Wright 2011); other populations differ |
| 30-Second Sit-to-Stand | Lower limb strength/endurance | Knee OA, hip OA, geriatric | Major clinically important improvement 2.0–2.6 repetitions in hip OA (Wright 2011) |
| 6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT) | Aerobic capacity, endurance | Deconditioning, stenosis, cardiac rehab | 50 meters (threshold not validated; used as a guide) |
| Single Leg Stance | Balance, proprioception | Ankle instability, fall risk | 5 seconds (threshold not validated; used as a guide) |
| Y-Balance Test | Dynamic balance, asymmetry | Lower limb, return-to-sport | Cut-off: anterior reach asymmetry ≤4 cm (derived with the Star Excursion Balance Test in high-school basketball players; Plisky 2006) |
| Stair Climb Test | Functional lower limb capacity | Knee/hip conditions | Time or step count improvement |
| Grip Strength | Hand/forearm function | Hand conditions, general health marker | 6 kg (threshold not validated; used as a guide) |
| Hop Tests (single, triple, crossover) | Power, confidence, symmetry | ACL rehab, return-to-sport | Criterion: >90% limb symmetry index (pass criterion, not an MCID; Grindem 2016) |

## Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (PROMs)

### Region-Specific PROMs

| Measure | Region | Items | MCID | Scoring |
|---------|--------|-------|------|---------|
| Neck Disability Index (NDI) | Cervical | 10 | MDC 5–10 points; reported MCID 5–19 points (MacDermid 2009) | 0-50 (higher = worse) |
| Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) | Lumbar | 10 | 10 points or 30% from baseline (Ostelo 2008) | 0-100% (higher = worse) |
| DASH (Disabilities of Arm, Shoulder, Hand) | Upper limb | 30 | 10 points | 0-100 (higher = worse) |
| SPADI (Shoulder Pain & Disability Index) | Shoulder | 13 | MCID 8–13 points; MDC about 18 points (Roy 2009) | 0-100 (higher = worse) |
| KOOS (Knee Injury & OA Outcome Score) | Knee | 42 | 8-10 points (threshold not validated; used as a guide) | 0-100 (higher = better) |
| HOOS (Hip Disability & OA Outcome Score) | Hip | 40 | 8-10 points (threshold not validated; used as a guide) | 0-100 (higher = better) |
| FAAM (Foot & Ankle Ability Measure) | Foot/ankle | 29 | MCID 8 (ADL) / 9 (Sports) points; MDC 5.7 (ADL) / 12.3 (Sports) points (Martin 2005) | 0-100% (higher = better) |
| LEFS (Lower Extremity Functional Scale) | Lower limb | 20 | 9 points | 0-80 (higher = better) |
| HDI (Henry Ford Hospital Headache Disability Inventory) | Headache | 25 | 29 points (change beyond test–retest error; Jacobson 1994) | 0-100 (higher = worse) |

### Generic PROMs

| Measure | Application | MCID |
|---------|-------------|------|
| Visual Analog Scale (VAS) | Pain intensity | 1.5-2.0 cm |
| Numeric Pain Rating Scale (NPRS) | Pain intensity | 2 points |
| Patient-Specific Functional Scale (PSFS) | Any — patient-selected activities | 1.2 points in upper-extremity problems (Hefford 2012); other populations differ |
| Global Rating of Change (GRoC) | Overall perceived change | ≥+3 (much better) (threshold not validated; used as a guide) |
| Tampa Scale of Kinesiophobia (TSK) | Fear of movement | 4 points |
| Pain Catastrophizing Scale (PCS) | Catastrophizing | 6 points (threshold not validated; used as a guide) |
| EQ-5D | Health-related quality of life | 0.074 (mean MID, utility score; Walters & Brazier 2005) |

| Tool | Purpose | When to Use | Action |

| STarT Back Screening Tool | Stratify LBP risk | All LBP cases at intake | High risk → consider psychology referral |
| Orebro Musculoskeletal Pain Questionnaire | Psychosocial risk | Chronic pain, non-response | High risk → multidisciplinary approach |

## How Outcome Measures Inform Decisions

```
Baseline Assessment
        ↓
    TRIAL (4 sessions)
        ↓
    Re-measure outcomes
        ↓
    Any continuation criterion met? ──── Yes → CONTINUE
        │
        No
        ↓
    Hypothesis revision
        ↓
    ONE new TRIAL with revised hypothesis (4 sessions)
        ↓
    Re-measure again
        ↓
    Any continuation criterion met? ──── Yes → CONTINUE
        │
        No
        ↓
    DISCHARGE or REFER
```

## Important Notes

1. **PROMs supplement, not replace, objective measures** — See [Subjective Report Insufficiency](models/subjective-report-insufficiency.md)
2. **Use the right tool for the right condition** — Don't use KOOS for a neck problem
3. **Track the same measures consistently** — Don't switch tools mid-treatment
4. **Document threshold status explicitly** — "Cervical rotation improved 12% (above MDC) → objective criterion met → CONTINUE; NDI improved 8 points (supporting, not sufficient alone)"
5. **Missing data must be documented** — If a measure cannot be taken, record why
